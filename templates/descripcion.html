Machine Learning Project

AlertaFiscal

A Machine Learning module that helps small businesses plan their tax burden. It forecasts a company's monthly income and expenses for the next 6 months, so the business can estimate how much cash to reserve for taxes before the obligations are due.

Problem

Small businesses pay VAT, income tax, and withholdings out of the same cash they use to operate. They usually find out how much they owe when the filing date arrives, not months before. When income drops or expenses rise unexpectedly, the money for taxes has not been set aside, and the business must borrow, pay late, or pay interest.

How can a Machine Learning model, integrated into a web application, forecast a small business's monthly income and expenses accurately enough to plan its tax reserve?

General Objective

Develop a time-series forecasting module, deployed in a Flask application, that estimates a company's monthly income and expenses for a 6-month horizon and turns that forecast into a tax-reserve plan with explanatory alerts.

Specific Objectives

  1. Analyze the open data from the Superintendencia de Sociedades (Colombian Superintendency of Companies) and the DIAN (Colombian tax authority) to obtain real sector margins, growth rates, and seasonality.
  2. Build a documented, reproducible, semi-synthetic dataset of 500 small businesses with 36 months of income and expenses each.
  3. Train forecasting models and compare them against a seasonal baseline using MAE and RMSE.
  4. Integrate the best model into the Flask application, publish it on Render, and log every forecast for monitoring.

How We Measure Success

MAE (main)
Average size of the error in millions of COP: "on average, the forecast misses by X million per month".
RMSE (main)
Like MAE, but large misses weigh more. It reveals a few very bad forecasts.
MAPE (secondary)
Error as a percentage of the real value. Unreliable when real values are close to zero.

Baseline: the model must beat a seasonal naive forecast ("same value as the same month last year"). Business criterion (proposed): the average monthly error must stay below 15 % of the company's average monthly income.

Real vs Simulated Data

No public dataset contains monthly figures per company, so the training data is semi-synthetic: annual company aggregates, sector margins, and the shape of seasonality are real; every monthly value per company is generated by a program calibrated with those public statistics. Results show that the method works technically; they do not prove accuracy for a real business.

Scope

Preventive support for academic purposes, not accounting or tax advice. It does not classify companies by tax risk, predict penalties, compute or file tax returns, or connect to the DIAN, and it does not replace an accountant. Every forecast is an estimate that must be reviewed by the owner or their accountant.
