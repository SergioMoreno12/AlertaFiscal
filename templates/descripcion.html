Machine Learning Project

AlertaFiscal

A Machine Learning module for the preventive management of tax compliance risk in Colombian SMEs. It estimates a risk score from 0 to 100, displays it as a traffic light, and explains which factors raise it.

Problem

SMEs must regularly manage their invoicing, VAT, withholding taxes, and other tax obligations. When this information is reviewed only manually, delays and atypical variations go unnoticed until they turn into interest, penalties, or tax authority requests. In addition, there is no public, labeled dataset on tax noncompliance by company.

How can a Machine Learning model, integrated into a web application, identify patterns associated with a higher risk of tax noncompliance in SMEs?

General Objective

Develop a Machine Learning module, deployed in a Flask application, that classifies an SME's tax compliance risk as low, medium, or high and presents it as a score, a traffic light, and explanatory alerts.

Specific Objectives

  1. Analyze the open data from the Superintendencia de Sociedades (Colombian Superintendency of Companies) and the DIAN (Colombian tax authority) to obtain reference financial and tax distributions.
  2. Build a documented, reproducible, semi-synthetic dataset with a three-class target variable.
  3. Train and compare logistic regression, a decision tree, Random Forest, and an MLP neural network.
  4. Integrate the best model into the Flask application and publish it on Render.

Risk Score and Traffic Light

The classifier's probabilities are converted into a score: score = (0.5 × P(medium) + 1.0 × P(high)) × 100

0 – 33
Low risk
Obligations up to date and stable behavior.
34 – 66
Medium risk
Some delay or atypical variation worth reviewing.
67 – 100
High risk
Several overdue obligations or pending withholdings.

Models to Compare

  • Multiclass logistic regression (baseline)
  • Decision tree
  • Random Forest
  • MLP neural network (32 and 16 neurons, ReLU, Dropout 0.20, Softmax)

Scope

Minimum viable product for academic purposes. It does not calculate or file tax returns, is not integrated with the DIAN, and does not guarantee outcomes regarding real penalties: it is a statistical estimate that must be reviewed by an accountant.
