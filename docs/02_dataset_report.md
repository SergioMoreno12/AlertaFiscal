## 2. Treatment of data problems

| Problem | Treatment | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Supersociedades values are rounded to 0.01 trillion COP** (COP 10,000 million). 49.9 % of rows have income below 0.05 trillion (quantized), and most profits are `0.00` or `0.01`. | Rows below 0.05 trillion are flagged as quantized. Margins and growth are computed **only** from records with income ≥ 0.5 trillion, where profit rounding moves the margin by at most 1 percentage point. | 2,679 precise rows (5.4 %). Margins are reliable, but they come from the largest companies (see limitations). |
| **Expenses are not reported** by Supersociedades. | Expenses = income − profit. | Expenses are consistent with the real margin of each record. |
| **Absurd values** (one-off events such as mergers or liquidations). | Margins outside −50 % to +60 % and YoY growth outside −50 % to +100 % are excluded from the sector statistics. | 93 of 2,679 margins and 30 of 1,857 growth pairs excluded. |
| **DIAN VAT is filed every two months**, in the month after each period, so collection jumps in odd months. | Each month's share of its year's total is computed, the median is taken across years, each filing pair (Jan–Feb, Mar–Apr, …) is averaged, and the curve is shifted back 2 months (collection in March–April reflects sales of January–February). | 12 smooth factors that average 1.0, from 0.79 (January–February) to 1.23 (November–December). |
| **DIAN is national, never per company.** | Used only for the *shape* of seasonality (relative factors). DIAN amounts are never used as any company's income, expenses or VAT. | No company value comes from DIAN totals. |
| **COVID distorts 2020–2021.** | Seasonality uses complete years 2015–2019 and 2022–2025 only. | The yearly shape reflects normal years. |
| **Supersociedades covers only the largest companies.** | Only relative quantities (margin, growth, size rank) are kept. Every company is rescaled to small-business size (section 3). | Values match a small business; the shape of each profile comes from real data. |
| **Encoding:** one sector name arrives with a broken accent (`CONSTRUCCI�N`). | Sector names are matched by prefix and translated to lowercase English. | All 50,000 rows get a valid sector. |

## 3. Generation method and parameters

For each company *c* and month *t* (0 to 35):

```
income[c,t]   = base_monthly_income[c] × (1 + growth[c])^(t/12) × seasonal[c,t] × shift[c,t] × exp(noise_income)
expenses[c,t] = income[c,t] × (1 − margin[c]) × exp(noise_expenses)
seasonal[c,t] = 1 + amplitude[c] × (factor[month_of_year(t)] − 1)
```

| Parameter | Value | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Random seed | 42 (`numpy.random.default_rng`) | Fixed in `generate_series.py` and `split_series.py` |
| Share of `derived` companies | 60 % per sector (capped by the eligible records) | Contract proposal |
| `margin` | Derived: the real record's margin. Simulated: Normal(sector median, sector spread). Clipped to −10 % … +40 %. | `sector_profiles.csv`, `companies_base.csv` |
| `growth` (annual) | Derived: the record's real YoY growth. Simulated: Normal(sector median, sector spread). Clipped to −30 % … +50 %. | Same |
| Size | Annual income between COP 300 million and COP 6,000 million, on a log scale, keeping the company's size rank (derived: rank among eligible real records; simulated: random). | Small-business range chosen by the team |
| **Rescaling factor** (derived) | small-business monthly income ÷ real monthly income. Median 1.26 × 10⁻³ (about 1 : 800), range 2.3 × 10⁻⁴ to 1.8 × 10⁻³. Stored per company in `company_profiles.csv`. | Computed |
| Seasonal factors | 12 values from `seasonality.csv` | DIAN |
| Seasonal amplitude | Uniform 0.6 – 1.4 per company | Generator |
| Income noise | Log-normal, σ uniform 0.04 – 0.10 per company | Generator |
| Expense noise | Additional log-normal, σ uniform 0.02 – 0.05 per company | Generator |
| Level shifts (hard cases) | 72 companies (≈ 15 %) get a permanent ±10–25 % change starting at a random month between 7 and 34 | Generator |

**Is the generator too easy?** A seasonal naive forecast ("same month last year") has a median error of **16.2 %** of the company's average monthly income on the test months (90th percentile: 34.6 %). The noise and level shifts keep the problem non-trivial while seasonality is still visible.

## 4. Split design

| Split | Rule | Companies | Rows |
|---|---|---|---|
| `train` | Months 1–24 (2023-01 to 2024-12) of non-holdout companies | 425 | 10,200 |
| `validation` | Months 25–30 (2025-01 to 2025-06) | 425 | 2,550 |
| `test` | Months 31–36 (2025-07 to 2025-12) | 425 | 2,550 |
| `holdout` | All 36 months of 75 companies never used in training | 75 | 2,700 |

Holdout companies per sector (at least one per sector, the rest proportional to sector size): agriculture 4, construction 6, manufacturing 15, mining 2, retail 27, services 21.

**Leakage prevention:** sector margins and growth come only from Supersociedades, never from the generated series; the temporal split keeps the future out of training; holdout companies never appear in `train`, `validation` or `test`; `company_profiles.csv` (true parameters) must never be used as model input.

## 6. Limitations

- **Semi-synthetic data.** The monthly values are generated. Results prove that the method works technically, not that the forecast is accurate for a real business.
- **Margins come from large companies.** Only records with income ≥ 0.5 trillion COP have precise margins. Small businesses may have different margins; the clipping to −10 % … +40 % keeps them plausible.
- **Thin margins produce loss months.** Retail's real median margin is 1.8 %, so with noise about 30 % of all company-months have expenses above income (retail 34 %, construction 18 %). This is realistic for low-margin businesses, but the alert "projected loss month" will fire often; Phase 1 may want to tune it.
- **One seasonal shape for all sectors.** DIAN collection is national; sectors differ only through each company's random amplitude.
- **Short history.** 36 months gives only three observations of each calendar month.
- **No real validation.** Testing with anonymized, authorized data from real small businesses is future work.
