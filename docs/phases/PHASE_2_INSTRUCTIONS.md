## 3. Glossary
- **Raw data:** original files, never edited by hand (Supersociedades CSV and DIAN Excel).
- **Derived:** values or parameters that come from real data (a real company's sector, margin or growth).
- **Simulated:** values created by the random generator.
- **Semi-synthetic dataset:** monthly series generated by a program, shaped by real statistics.
- **Seasonality factor:** number per calendar month that says how far that month is above or below the yearly average (1.10 means 10% above).
- **YoY growth:** year over year change, for example income of 2024 divided by income of 2023, minus 1.
- **NIT:** Colombian tax id. Repeated NITs across years let us measure real growth.
- **Random seed:** a fixed number that makes a random generator give the same result every run.
- **Holdout companies:** about 15% of companies reserved so the model never sees them in training.
- **Data leakage:** information from validation, test or the future reaching training.

## 4. The data contract (freeze November 1, 2026)

**File `ml/data/processed/monthly_series.csv`: 6 columns, 36 rows per company, 500 companies (18,000 rows).**

| Column | Type | Unit | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| `company_id` | text | none | `C0001` to `C0500`, never reused |
| `sector` | text | none | Lowercase English name, for example `retail`, `manufacturing`, `services` |
| `month` | text | `YYYY-MM` | 36 consecutive months, `2023-01` to `2025-12`, same for all companies |
| `income` | number | millions of COP | Zero or positive, no empty values, two decimals |
| `expenses` | number | millions of COP | Zero or positive, no empty values, two decimals |
| `origin` | text | none | `derived` or `simulated`, same value for all 36 rows of a company |

**`origin` rule (decision to confirm with the team):** `derived` means the company's profile (sector, margin, annual growth) was taken from a real Supersociedades record; the monthly values are always generated. `simulated` means the profile was sampled from the sector's distributions. Both are rescaled to small-business size, and the factor is documented.

**File `ml/data/processed/split_manifest.csv`: 4 columns, one row per company and month.**

| Column | Type | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `company_id` | text | Same ids as the series |
| `month` | text | `YYYY-MM` |
| `month_index` | integer | 1 to 36 |
| `split` | text | `train` (months 1-24), `validation` (25-30), `test` (31-36), or `holdout` |

75 of the 500 companies (15%) are `holdout`: all 36 of their rows are marked `holdout`. Holdout companies are chosen randomly inside each sector, so every sector has some.

## 6. What you must do, step by step

**Step 1. Confirm the contract (by November 1, 2026).** Check the four proposals in this document (calendar, size, `origin` rule, rescaling). Tell Sergio the result the same day so he writes the dictionary. After November 1, 2026, nothing in section 4 changes.

**Step 2. `ml/src/prepare_data.py`: clean the raw data.**
- Supersociedades: read the CSV, standardize column names, convert amounts to millions of COP, treat the quantized values below 0.05 billion COP (document how: flag them and exclude them from margin and growth calculations), keep NIT, sector, income and expenses per year.
- Compute per sector: median margin and its spread.
- Compute real YoY growth per NIT using companies that appear in more than one year (2021-2025). Summarize growth per sector (median and spread). Remove absurd values and document the rule you used.
- DIAN: read the Excel and extract only the monthly shape. Smooth it over 2-month windows because of bimonthly VAT filing. Output 12 seasonality factors that average 1.0. Never use DIAN totals as a company's VAT.
- Outputs in `ml/data/processed/`: `sector_profiles.csv` (margin and growth per sector), `seasonality.csv` (12 factors), `companies_base.csv` (the real records used for `derived` companies).

**Step 3. `ml/src/generate_series.py`: generate the monthly series.**
- Use a fixed seed (42) so the output is identical every run.
- Create 500 companies. About 60% `derived` (profile from a real record), 40% `simulated` (profile sampled from sector distributions). Rescale all to small-business size and document the factor.
- For each company and month: income = base level × growth trend × seasonality factor × random noise. Expenses = income × (1 − margin) with its own noise, so expenses follow income but not perfectly.
- Rules: no negative values, round to two decimals, 36 months, noise small enough to keep seasonality visible but large enough that the forecast is not trivial.
- Output: `ml/data/processed/monthly_series.csv` with the exact 6 columns and order of section 4.

**Step 4. `ml/src/split_series.py`: build the manifest.**
- Choose 75 holdout companies at random, spread across sectors (use the same seed).
- For the rest, mark months 1-24 `train`, 25-30 `validation`, 31-36 `test`.
- Output: `ml/data/processed/split_manifest.csv`.

**Step 5. `ml/src/check_dataset.py`: validate everything.** It must fail loudly if any rule is broken. Checks:
- 6 columns, exact names and order,
- 500 companies, 36 rows each, 18,000 rows in total,
- months from `2023-01` to `2025-12`, no gaps,
- no nulls, no negatives,
- `origin` has only the two allowed values and is constant per company,
- manifest has the same keys as the series,
- 75 holdout companies, all with 36 `holdout` rows,
- holdout companies have no `train`, `validation` or `test` rows,
- running twice produces identical files (compare a hash).
